Cost to Build a Deck in Knoxville (2026 Guide)

Updated May 25, 2026 · 6 min read

In 2026, building a deck in Knoxville typically costs $30–$60 per square foot for pressure-treated wood, $55–$95 per square foot for composite, and $100+ per square foot for premium hardwood or multi-level designs. A common 300–400 sq ft deck usually lands between $12,000 and $30,000 installed, depending on material, height off the ground, railings, and features. Below is how those numbers break down and what moves them.

Deck Cost by Material (Knoxville, 2026)

Material is the single biggest factor in deck cost. Here are typical installed price ranges for the East Tennessee market:

What Drives the Price Up or Down

Two decks of the same size can differ in price by thousands of dollars. The biggest variables in the Knoxville area are:

  • Height off the ground — elevated decks on sloped East TN lots need taller posts, bigger footings, and often stairs, all of which add cost.
  • Railing system — basic wood rail is cheapest; aluminum, cable, and glass railings cost more but last longer and look cleaner.
  • Footings & framing — frost-depth footings and proper ledger flashing are required by code and protect your home from water damage.
  • Shape & levels — multi-level decks, angles, and built-in seating or planters increase labor.
  • Add-ons — stairs, lighting, pergolas, and screened enclosures each add to the total.

Permits & Code in Knox County

Decks attached to a home or above a certain height require a building permit in Knox County and the City of Knoxville, and must meet IRC code for footing depth, ledger attachment, railing height (36" residential), and baluster spacing (4" max). StructureWorks pulls the permits and builds to code so your deck passes inspection and protects your home. For a broader overview, see our Knox County permit guide.

Composite vs. Wood in East Tennessee's Climate

Tennessee's humid summers and freeze-thaw winters are hard on wood. Pressure-treated lumber is the lowest upfront cost but needs annual sealing and will eventually warp, crack, or splinter. Composite decking costs more initially but shrugs off moisture and insects, never needs staining, and commonly lasts 25+ years — which is why many Knoxville homeowners choose it as the better long-term value.
